JH-1.04 · Chapter 1: The Supreme Lord

Name 4 of 54

भूतभव्यभवत्प्रभुः பூதபவ்யபவத்ப்ரபு: Bhūtabhavyabhavatprabhuḥ

The Master of the past, the present, and the future.

Acharya's Teaching

Bhūta means the past, bhavya the present, bhavat the future, and prabhu means master. Because He alone rules over all three divisions of time — yesterday, today, and tomorrow — only He can grant a boon that exceeds the limits time places on an ordinary lifetime, as He did for Romaśa, whose lifespan was tied to every hair on his own body.

The Story

Dr. Venkatesh tells of the sage Romaśa, whose body was covered in bear-like hair. Romaśa asked an astrologer for a life longer than Brahmā's — not out of greed, but so he could visit and savor every festival at each of the 108 Divya Deśams unhurriedly, again and again, rather than rushing through them as a checklist.

The astrologer told him only the Lord who rules past, present, and future could grant a boon beyond time itself, and gave him the mantra Bhūta Bhavya Bhavat Prabhave Namaḥa to recite daily. Perumāḷ appeared and granted the boon: for every lifetime of Brahmā that passed, one hair would fall from Romaśa's body — and he would live until every hair had fallen.

Living This Divine Name

When we feel rushed to “finish” our devotion like a checklist, this name reminds us the Lord who holds all time is never in a hurry either.

Today's Reflection & Practice

Slow down one routine act of devotion today, and let it be unhurried.

Chant

Om Bhūta Bhavya Bhavat Prabhave Namaḥa

Connection to the Journey Home

The soul's journey home is not a race — the Master of all three times welcomes the jivatma however long the path takes.
