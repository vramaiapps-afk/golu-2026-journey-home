JH-4.03 · Chapter 4: The Guide

Name 30 of 54

निधिरव्ययः அவ்யயநிதிஃ Nidhiravyayaḥ

The imperishable Treasure, the one wealth that can never be stolen or destroyed.

Acharya's Teaching

Nidhi means treasure; avyayaḥ means that which never perishes or diminishes at any time. Dr. Venkatesh pairs this with the Āḷvārs' own experience: however vast one's worldly wealth, it is destined to end one day, but Perumāḷ alone is the imperishable treasure that pours out undiminished bliss and liberation to His devotees across every age, never running dry.

The Story

Dr. Venkatesh tells of Kubera and Rāvaṇa. Kubera, lord of the north and king of the nine treasuries, possessed boundless wealth — the śaṅkha Nidhi, the Padma Nidhi, every treasure imaginable. His half-brother Rāvaṇa, consumed by arrogance, attacked Kubera, seized his Puṣpaka Vimāna, and plundered every treasure he owned.

Grief-stricken, Kubera went to sage Nārada and cried: "I was lord of all wealth in the world, and my own brother has reduced me to nothing! Is no worldly treasure permanent?" Nārada explained: every worldly treasure is perishable — stolen by thieves, eroded by time, passed from hand to hand. But there is one treasure no one can ever plunder, one wealth that never diminishes: the lotus feet of Śrīman Nārāyaṇa Himself. Nārada counseled Kubera to take refuge in Him alone. Following this, Kubera worshipped Perumāḷ and found, in Him, the one treasure that could never be taken from him again.

Living This Divine Name

Every worldly treasure we hold can be taken from us — by time, by circumstance, by someone else's greed. Only what we build in devotion can never be plundered.

Today's Reflection & Practice

Name one thing you treasure today that could be lost, and one that never can be.

Chant

Om Avyayanidhayē Namaḥa

Connection to the Journey Home

Kubera learned that even a king of treasuries can be robbed of everything but one — the same undiminishing treasure the soul finds waiting at the end of its journey home, never to be taken away again.
