JH-3.06 · Chapter 3: The Protector

Name 24 of 54

पुरुषोत्तमः புருஷோத்தமஃ Puruṣottamaḥ

The One supreme above every category of soul — bound, liberated, and eternal.

Acharya's Teaching

Dr. Venkatesh lays out three categories of soul: Puruṣas — bound souls still living on earth; Puruṣottu (Muktas) — souls liberated after living here and surrendering; and Puruṣottarar (Nityas) — eternal souls like Garuḍa and Ādiśeṣa who never entered saṃsāra at all. Because Perumāḷ is superior to all three categories — as He proved even to the eternal, exalted Garuḍa — He is Puruṣottamaḥ, supreme above every kind of being.

The Story

Dr. Venkatesh tells of Garuḍa's sworn enemy, the snake Sumukhan, fleeing to heaven and hiding near the throne of Perumāḷ (as Upendra). Garuḍa demands his prey; Perumāḷ refuses — He never abandons anyone who has taken refuge at His feet. Furious, Garuḍa boasts of his own strength, noting he carries Perumāḷ on his shoulders daily — surely proof he is the stronger of the two.

Perumāḷ smiles and presses just His left hand down on Garuḍa's shoulder — and Garuḍa cannot bear even that weight alone. Realizing his error, Garuḍa admits that the very strength to carry Perumāḷ was itself Perumāḷ's gift to him, not his own. He is then told to carry the very snake he wanted to kill on his own shoulder from then on — depicted today at Thiruvellarai as a sculpture titled “Garuḍa's pride destroyed.”

Living This Divine Name

What we take pride in as “our own” strength often has a source beyond us worth acknowledging.

Today's Reflection & Practice

Credit one strength of yours today to where it truly came from.

Chant

Om Puruṣottamāya Namaḥa

Connection to the Journey Home

Even the eternal attendants of Vaikuṇṭha owe their strength to Him — a reminder that the soul's journey home ends not in self-sufficiency, but in recognizing the Source of everything, always.
