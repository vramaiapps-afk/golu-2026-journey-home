JH-1.07 · Chapter 1: The Supreme Lord

Name 7 of 54

भावः பாவ: Bhāvaḥ

The One who unfurls all worlds from within Himself, like a peacock opening its tail.

Acharya's Teaching

Dr. Venkatesh explains this name through the image of a peacock's tail: folded, it looks ordinary, but unfurled, all its intrinsic beauty is suddenly visible. Before creation, all the worlds exist folded and compact within Perumāḷ, just as the peacock's feathers exist compact before opening. At the moment of creation — and, as this story shows, whenever He chooses — He unfurls what was always folded within Him, and the worlds spring forth. Bhāvaḥ means the One who causes all worlds to arise, or unfold, from Himself.

The Story

Dr. Venkatesh continues the story of Kṛṣṇa in Duryodhana's court: to trap Kṛṣṇa, Duryodhana had Him seated over a hidden pit of wrestlers, planning to drop Him in when the cloth beneath the seat was pulled. When Kṛṣṇa declares the Pāṇḍavas dearer to Him than His own life, Duryodhana in fury has the cloth pulled — but instead of falling, Kṛṣṇa reveals His Viśvarūpa.

Every world and being, folded within Him, suddenly appears — just as a peacock's hidden beauty is seen only once its tail unfurls. Because He brings forth all worlds from within Himself this way, He is called Bhāvaḥ.

Living This Divine Name

What looks ordinary and folded-up in us may hold beauty and vastness we haven't yet shown the world.

Today's Reflection & Practice

Let one hidden part of yourself show today, rather than staying folded away.

Chant

Om Bhāvāya Namaḥa

Connection to the Journey Home

The vastness Perumāḷ revealed to Duryodhana is the same vastness the soul beholds on reaching Paramapadam.
