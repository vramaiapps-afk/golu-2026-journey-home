JH-2.08 · Chapter 2: The Compassionate Lord

Name 17 of 54

अक्षरः அக்ஷரஃ Akṣaraḥ

The One who never diminishes — an ever-constant, unfading light.

Acharya's Teaching

Kṣaram means to diminish or decay; Akṣaraḥ means without diminishment — ever full, never wanting. Having cured the Moon's own waning affliction, Perumāḷ Himself remains the opposite of what He healed: a constant, unwaning light, never subject to the cycles of increase and decrease that govern everything else in creation, including the very moon that bears His grace.

The Story

Dr. Venkatesh tells of Chandra (the Moon), married to all 27 daughters of Dakṣa Prajāpati — the 27 nakṣatras — who favors only Rohiṇī and neglects the rest. Enraged, Dakṣa curses Chandra with a wasting disease, and Chandra begins losing his kalas one by one — the origin of the waning moon — until nothing remains at Amāvāsyā.

Begging for release, Chandra points out to Dakṣa that the curse harms Dakṣa's own daughters too, since they are bound to Chandra. Dakṣa relents and directs him to Mayilāduthurai (Thiruindalūr), where Parimala Raṅganāthan resides. Bathing in the temple's Indu Puṣkariṇi tank and worshipping the deity there, Chandra regains his kalas one by one — the waxing moon — until he shines full again. Āḷvār describes Perumāḷ here as a "nandā vilakku" — a lamp that never dims — in contrast to the very moon He healed.

Living This Divine Name

Where the world around us waxes and wanes, He remains completely, reliably constant.

Today's Reflection & Practice

Return today to one steady truth about Him when things around you feel uncertain.

Chant

Om Akṣarāya Namaḥa

Connection to the Journey Home

Where the journey itself has stages, phases, and changing light, the destination — Paramapadam, and the Lord who awaits there — never diminishes or changes.
