JH-2.05 · Chapter 2: The Compassionate Lord

Name 14 of 54

पुरुषः புருஷஃ Puruṣaḥ

The One who gives Himself entirely to those who love Him.

Acharya's Teaching

Puruṣaḥ means the One who gives Himself entirely to those who love Him — Dr. Venkatesh notes the same root gives Tamil its word for husband (puruṣan), one who gives himself wholly to his wife, keeping nothing back for himself. Just as Perumāḷ left the princess's lap the instant Rāmānuja called, and later refused to be separated from her devotion, He gives Himself completely to whoever truly loves Him — regardless of how imperfect or unknowing that love may be.

The Story

Dr. Venkatesh tells of Rāmānuja visiting the palace of the Sultan of Delhi to reclaim a deity taken during a raid on Thirunārāyaṇapuram. Shown every looted idol, Rāmānuja calls out, "Selvapillāi vārāi!" ("Come, my dear child!") to each, but none respond. Only one place remains unchecked: the Sultan's young daughter, who plays in her chambers with what she believes is a doll.

Unable to enter the women's quarters, Rāmānuja calls from outside — and the deity runs straight from the princess's lap into his. Reinstalling Him at Thirunārāyaṇapuram, Rāmānuja notices tears during the consecration and asks why. Perumāḷ explains: the princess loved Him sincerely, whether or not she knew Him as God or merely as a doll — and that love moved Him. Rāmānuja, understanding her devotion, could not bear to separate them, and brought her too, consecrating her as "Bibi Nāchiyār" between the deity's two feet, where she is honored to this day.

Living This Divine Name

Love this genuine doesn't go unnoticed — it draws Him completely to us, however small or uninformed that love may seem.

Today's Reflection & Practice

Express one moment of genuine, uncalculated love today.

Chant

Om Puruṣāya Namaḥa

Connection to the Journey Home

The Journey Home ends not in distance but in total union — the same completeness with which He gave Himself to Bibi Nāchiyār awaits the soul that reaches Him.
