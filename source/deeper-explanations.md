## The decad in one frame (use as an intro on the site)

- **What it is.** Nammāḻvār sits under the tamarind tree at Āḻvār Tirunagari, on the Tāmiraparaṇi bank. Perumāḷ is about to grant him moksha (the next decad, *Muniyē Nānmuganē*). Before that, He shows him, in the **mind's eye** (*mānasika anubhava*), how the journey to Vaikuṇṭha will go. It is a "trailer" given ahead of the real thing. Nothing in these ten verses is physical travel.
- **The refrain.** In every pasuram the Lord leads (*vazhi naḍatthi, kai-p-piḍitthu azhaitthu-c-cheygiRān*) the devotee along the **Archirādhi Mārgam** (the path of light). Each verse gives one **reason** why He does it. The ten reasons are listed in the table at the end.
- **Where Nammāḻvār is placed.** The "devotees" who receive all this honour are not only Nammāḻvār. He sings it generally (*nāraṇan tamar*, "servants of Nārāyaṇa") so that it applies to **every** prapanna who goes this way. Dr. Venkatesh stresses this in verses 1 and 2.
- **The 12 stages (lokas).** Dr. Venkatesh counts them in verses 4–7 using the Chāndogya Upaniṣad list (*arcisham-evābhisambhavanti…*) and Nāḍādūr Ammāḷ's śloka (*muktārcir-dina-pūrvapakṣa-ṣaḍudaṅ-māsābda-vātāṁśumat-klau-vidyud-varuṇendra-dhātṛ…*): **Agni, Day (Dina), Bright fortnight (Śukla Pakṣa), Uttarāyaṇa, Year (Saṁvatsara), Vāyu, Sūrya (Āditya), Chandra, Vidyut (lightning), Varuṇa, Indra, Brahmā/Prajāpati (Satyaloka)**. After these twelve comes the **Virajā river**, the boundary between *līlā vibhūti* (the material universe) and *nitya vibhūti* (Vaikuṇṭha).
- **Who leads.** The *Ātivāhikas* ("those who carry across", each a deity of one stage) guide the soul stage by stage. Dr. Venkatesh also says Perumāḷ Himself often walks with the soul ("for some, the Lord Himself comes and holds their hand… a separate matter").

## Pasuram 1 — *Sūzh visumbu aṇi mugil*
**Reason given: "En Appan" — the Lord is the soul's *father*, and a father leads his child by the hand.**

**Verse (from recitation):**
சூழ்விசும்பு அணிமுகில் தூரியம் முழக்கின
ஆழ்கடல் அலைதிரை கையெடுத்து ஆடின
ஏழ்பொழிலும் வளம் ஏந்திய என்னப்பன்
வாழ்புகழ் நாரணன் தமரைக் கண்டுகந்தே

**Deeper explanation**
- *Sūzh visumbu* = the sky that surrounds the earth on all sides, without a visible beginning or end. *Aṇi mugil* = the clouds, which are the sky's *ornament*, as earrings, bangles and a chain are ours.
- *Tūriyam* is an auspicious (*maṅgaḷa*) instrument played at ceremonies. Clouds cannot play instruments; their only sound is thunder. At the departure of a devotee, that thunder *becomes* the maṅgaḷa-tūriyam. Dr. Venkatesh: it is "a trailer before the main picture". Before Nammāḻvār hears the voice of the cloud-coloured Lord (*mēgha-vaṇṇan*), he hears the voice of the clouds.
- *Aḻ-kaḍal alai-tirai kai-yeduttu āḍina*: the waves (*alai* = that which keeps moving, *tirai* = wave) are the sea's *hands*, stretched out and drawn back like a Bharatanāṭyam dancer's abhinaya. The sea wishes to dance, and the waves are its hands. It is also a trailer: Nammāḻvār is about to plunge into the ocean of auspicious qualities (*guṇa-kaḍal*), and the sea comes with arms raised to embrace him, as the Lord will.
- *Ēḻ pozhil*: *pozhil* here means islands (*dvīpa*), the seven dvīpas known to the ṛṣis: Jambū, Plakṣa, Śālmali, Kuśa, Krauñca, Śāka, Puṣkara. *Vaḷam* = gifts/offerings (*upakāram*), like flowers, dry fruits or ghee we carry to a temple. The deity in charge of each dvīpa brings gifts.
- **Why do they do this for one soul?** Because *en appan*. Nammāḻvār says: I have a Father, Nārāyaṇa. The clouds, seas and islands are the King's servants. As the King's ministers bow to the prince walking in the street *because he is the king's son*, so they honour the child of the Lord.
- *Vāzh-pugazh nāraṇan*: *pugazh* = auspicious qualities; *vāzh* = those qualities that make the devotee *live* (flourish), here and there.
- ***Nāraṇan tamar* — "the Lord's servants" in general.** He does not say "me". Whoever goes to moksha gets this. And *ukantē*: nobody ordered it; it was **spontaneous joy** (*prīti*). The Lord said one word ("*gaṭṭi-mēḷam*") and the creation answered not out of command but because "to serve him is our fortune".

## Pasuram 2 — *Nāraṇan tamarai kaṇḍu*
**Reason given: "Nārāyaṇan" — the support (*ayana*) of all souls; one who supports must also carry them on the path.**

**Verse (from recitation):**
நாரணன் தமரைக் கண்டுகந்து நன்னீர் முகில்
பூரணப் பொற்குடம் பூரித்தது உயர்விண்ணில்
நீரணிக் கடல்கள் நின்றார்த்தன
நெடுவரைத் தோரணம் நிறைத்தெங்கும் தொழுதனர் உலகே

**Deeper explanation**
- **Nārāyaṇa**: *nāra* = the collection of jīvātmās; *ayana* = support/abode. He is the ādhāra (support) of every soul, so He cannot leave them on the way.
- **A second meaning** Dr. Venkatesh relishes: *nāra* = water, *ayana* = dwelling: the One who lies on the waters (the pralaya waters / Kṣīrābdhi). The clouds are *nannīr mugil* ("clouds of good water"). So the water-clouds feel a **natural affinity** (*svābhāvika abhimāna*) with the devotee of the One who rests on water. That is why they are not satisfied with the first service (thunder in verse 1) and want to do another.
- The soul now leaves the body through the **mūrdhanya nāḍi** (the artery to the crown, *sushumnā*) and arrives first at **Agni loka**. The text (*arcisham-evābhisambhavanti*) puts Agni first. (Note: Dr. Venkatesh says only that "Agni loka" is the first stage; he does not describe a yajña fire there.)
- The devas want to honour Nammāḻvār with **pūrṇa-kumbham** but have none in the heavens. So they **carry the clouds themselves** and set them as golden pots (*pūraṇa-poṟkuḍam*) in the sky. *Pūritthadu* has a double sense: they became "like" pūrṇa-kumbhas, and the clouds were *delighted*. (He jokes: "we don't have a cot, so this is like a cot".)
- The sea sees the cloud has served twice (thunder, pot) and thinks it must serve twice as well. It already danced; now it **roars on the shore** to play the ceremonial music during the pūrṇa-kumbha honour (*nīr aṇi kaḍalgaḷ ninrārttana*), so two honours happen at once.
- *Neḍu varai tōraṇam*: *varai* = mountain. The pillars on which the toraṇam hangs are as tall as mountains. *Niṟaittu* = set firm and straight, lest they fall.
- *Tozhudanar ulagē*: the dwellers of the higher worlds fold their hands from all directions and beg Nammāḻvār: "Please place your golden feet in our world." **The ālvārs are higher than the devas**, he stresses, so devas feel honoured.

## Pasuram 3 — *Tozhudanar ulagargaḷ*
**Reason given: "Bhūmi andru aḷandavan" — Trivikrama, who once measured the worlds and placed His foot on every head.**

**Verse (from recitation):**
தொழுதனர் உலகர்கள் தூபநன் மலர்மழை
பொழிவனர் பூமி அன்று அளந்தவன் தமர்
முன்னே எழுமின் என்று இருமருங்கு இசைத்தனர்
முனிவர்கள் வழி இது வைகுந்தற்கு என்று வந்தெதிரே

**Deeper explanation**
- The Lord who measured the earth as Trivikrama **placed His foot on everyone's head**. That was the *spiritual medicine* (*āṉmīga vaidyam*) to ripen *bhakti* and *jñāna*. Because of that touch the soul now goes to Vaikuṇṭha. Perumāḷ's joy at the result is greater than ours: "this treatment of mine has worked", so He receives them out of eagerness.
- Here the **Ātivāhikas** appear ("*ulagar*" in this verse). Dr. Venkatesh's image: **Google Maps for the spiritual path**. They know each stage and hand the soul on to the next. They are sent by Perumāḷ.
- Their honours, in order: **fold hands** (*tozhudanar*); **incense** (*tūpam*) and **a rain of flowers** (*nal malar mazhai*) from the divine worlds; **praise** ("this one is a servant of the Lord who measured the world", *bhūmi andru aḷandavan tamar*).
- **Their manners** (Dr. Venkatesh stresses this): they do not stand in front with a "big-brother attitude" ("you don't know the way, hold my hand"). They first stand aside, offer *namaskāra*, incense and flowers, praise him, then come before him and say, "We have come to guide you, Sir." This also shows the ālvār is **higher than the Ātivāhikas**.
- ***Ezhumin*** = "please rise / graciously come" (*ezhundarulavēṇḍum*). They are not leading; they are *requesting*. *Irumarungu isaitthanar* = they stood on both sides and said it **in agreement of heart** (*isaintu*), not just spoken, with love.
- ***Munivargaḷ***: the question — are the Ātivāhikas "munis" (silent ones)? Nampiḷḷai (Īḍu) says *mauni* = one who is **silent**. Normally these guides only point and stay silent. For Nammāḻvār they broke their silence and praised him with their mouths. That is why they are called *munivar*.
- ***Vazhi idu Vaikuntharkku*** = "This is the way to Vaikuṇṭha." The image (Nampiḷḷai): a **king returning home from a foreign land**. His people know he knows the way, but after long separation they run out and cry "this way, this way, welcome home". Nammāḻvār's native place (*sondha ūr*) is Vaikuṇṭha, so the guides greet him as a returning king.

## Pasuram 4 — *Edhir edhir imaiyavar*
**Reason given: "Mādhavan" — specially Kṛṣṇa, who came down to mix with us for a short time; will He not lead us to live with Him forever?**

**Verse (from recitation):**
எதிரெதிர் இமையவர் இருப்பிடம் வகுத்தனர்
கதிரவர் அவரவர் கைந்நிறை காட்டினர்
அதிர்குரல் முரசங்கள் அலைகடல் முழக்கொத்த
மதுவிரி துழாய்முடி மாதவன் தமர்க்கே

**Deeper explanation**
- **Mādhavan** has two meanings: Lakṣmī's husband (see pasuram 5) and, as elders show, **Kṛṣṇa** specifically. Kṛṣṇa was born only to mix a little with us as a cowherd. He who comes down for a short association will certainly come to lead us to a permanent one.
- **Location:** between the Vāyu, Sūrya and Chandra lokas.
- ***Edhir edhir***: *edhir* here is not "opposite" but "**on the way**", as when we say "a friend came *edhir* (towards me) on the road". Along the route the devas set up *iruppidam* (resting places, *maṇḍapams*).
- ***Imaiyavar*** ("those who do not blink"): they did not sleep, did not even blink, building these halls all night, as **Lakṣmaṇa** kept watch and built the hut at Pañcavaṭī and Citrakūṭa.
- **Do they rest there?** No — the ālvār is in a hurry for Vaikuṇṭha. Nampiḷḷai's king analogy: when a king processes through a country, servants build maṇḍapams on the way. The king may not stay, but he sees them, is pleased, and praises them. So the devas hope for **one gracious glance** (*kaṭākṣam*). Nampiḷḷai compares the decoration to **Mahānavami** (Āyudha Pūjā), when every object in a house is decorated.
- ***Kadiravar*** = the **twelve Ādityas** (*dvādaśa-ādityas*). We see one sun; the śāstras say there are twelve. Their rays are **their hands**. They stretch out a thousand hands each in welcome, "and it does not scorch the ālvār" — they offer in humility, he accepts in grace. *Avaravar* = the other Ātivāhikas and their groups joined in.
- ***Kai nirai kāṭṭinar*** = they show with their whole hands, again and again, "look, look": the maṇḍapams, toraṇams, pūrṇa-kumbhams, Veda-pārāyaṇam, singing.
- ***Adir kural murasangaḷ alai-kaḍal muzhakkoṭṭa***: the war-drums (*murasu*) sound like the roar of the sea.
- ***Madhuviri tuzhāy mudi Mādhavan tamarkkē***: **this honour is for the devotees of the One who wears a crown of honey-dripping tulasī, as Lakṣmī's husband.** Two conditions, Dr. Venkatesh says, are *important*: (1) worshipping the Lord **with a divine form** (*tirumēni*), a head, a crown, a tulasī garland dripping with honey, i.e. with His beauty and auspicious qualities, **not** as an abstract "spirit" or "principle"; (2) worshipping **with the Divine Mother** (*Mādhavan*), not as a meditation aid to be dropped when the mind is steady. "Honour in the path of light is for those who worship the Lord *with* Śrī."
- **Consolation for Śrīvaiṣṇavas** (his story): Milagāzhvān was a scholar who was refused honours by a king *because* he was a Vaiṣṇava, and danced for joy: "he called me a Vaiṣṇava in open court — what greater reward?" Nampiḷḷai's point: any insult suffered on earth is **repaid on this path**.

## Pasuram 5 — *Mādhavan tamar endru vāsalil vānavar*
**Reason given: "Mādhavan" as Lakṣmī's husband — Piraṭṭi delights when the Lord leads her children home.**

**Verse (from recitation):**
மாதவன் தமரெனா வாசலில் வானவர்
போதுமின் எமதிடம் புகுதுக என்றலும்
கீதங்கள் பாடினர் கின்னரர் கருடர்கள்
வேதநல் வாயவர் வேள்வியுள் மடுத்தே

**Deeper explanation**
- **The reason (the fifth):** A wife is pleased with her husband when he **cares for the children**, even if he ignores her. So Piraṭṭi is pleased with Perumāḷ only when He leads His children (us) by the hand to Vaikuṇṭha. That is why Mādhavan takes each of us by the hand.
- **Stage:** the soul reaches the last of the three worlds — **Varuṇa, Indra, Brahmā (Prajāpati)**. Each deity waits **at the gate of his own world**.
- ***Mādhavan tamar*** — not "Keśavan tamar". Nammāḻvār is not only the Lord's servant but the servant of the Lord **together with Śrī**. Dr. Venkatesh points to the many places in Tiruvāymozhi where the ālvār sings of Śrī: *ahalagillēn iRaiyum enru alarmēl mangai uRai mārbā*, *tiruvin maṇāḷan*, *tirumangai tannōdu tigazhgindra tirumāl*. (Also *nin tiruvuṭai aḍigaḷ*.) He is a **śaraṇāgata who surrendered with Śrī**.
- ***Pōdumin*** = "please come" (not "go" — "pōdum" sounds like "enough/go"; here it is the courteous "come"). *Emadu idam pugudu* = "enter our place". They plead: "Since you are going to Vaikuṇṭha anyway through *our* world (the Ātivāhikas stand in the middle), please set your golden feet here as well."
- ***Kīdangaḷ pāḍinar kinnarar garuḍargaḷ***: *Kinnaras and Garuḍas* here are singers (as in *Tiruppaḷḷi-yeḻucci*: "kinnarar garuḍar"). They live in worlds adjacent to these (kinnara-loka, garuḍa-loka) that are *not on the Archirādhi path*, so they come and sing.
- ***Vēda nal vāyavar*** ("those with good voices for Veda"): in every loka the Lord has placed Vaidikas who chant the Vedas; only those well-versed in the Vedas can reach the higher worlds. ***Vēḷviyuḷ maduttē*** = **offering the fruit of their sacrifices** (*vēḷvi-uḷ* = fruit/puṇya of the yajña; *maduttal* = offering).
- **Objection answered:** Nammāḻvār is beyond karma and cannot receive the fruit of yajña. Why offer? Because they want to honour him, and **you offer your best possession**. A Vaidika's best possession is his yajña-phala. As we say *Kṛṣṇārpaṇam*, they treat Nammāḻvār *as God's sandal* (see verse 6) and make the offering at his feet. They gain *sattā* (fulfilment) by offering; **he is not affected by it**.

## Pasuram 6 — *Vāḷ oṇ kaṇ maḍandhaiyar*
**Reason given: "Āzhiyān" — He holds the Sudarśana cakra; *su-darśana* = the perfect *path*; the Lord who carries the guide of the path will guide us.**

**Verse (from recitation; the caption read *vāzh oṇ* — *vāḷ oṇ kaṇ* is the title form and fits the meaning):**
வேள்வியுள் மடுத்தலும் விரைகமழ் நறும்புகை
காளங்கள் வலம்புரி கலந்தெங்கும் இசைத்தனர்
ஆண்மின்கள் வானகம் ஆழியான் தமரெனா
வாள்நுண் கண் மடந்தையர் வாழ்த்தினர் மகிழ்ந்தே
*(Check: Dr. Venkatesh reads the third-fourth words as "vāḷ oṇ kaṇ" — "bright, beautiful eyes" — and the caption rendering "nuṇ" should be confirmed in print.)*

**Deeper explanation**
- **Sudarśana:** *darśana* = path / way of seeing. *Su-darśana* = the excellent path. The Lord keeps the Sudarśana cakra in hand because the Lord leads devotees along the path to Vaikuṇṭha. (He ties it to calling Rāmānuja's way *Rāmānuja Darśanam* — one who made the way straight.)
- **This is the twelfth stage.** By the end of this pasuram all **12 lokas** are crossed, the soul is leaving **Brahmā's Satyaloka**.
- **Three honours:** (1) yajña-phala offered at the feet (continuing 5); (2) **fragrant incense** (*virai-kamaz nuṟumpugai*) from another group of Ātivāhikas; (3) **instruments played alternately** (*kalandu*, Nampiḷḷai: "in turn") — the **kāḷam** (*tiruccinnam*, a Veda-ceremonial trumpet; Dr. Venkatesh recalls Deśika's *Tiruccinna-mālai* and the Varadarāja procession at Kāñcī), and the **valampuri** conch. Between the two sounds they praise "*Kurukūr Śaṭhakōpan vandār… Māran vandār…*", the titles of Nammāḻvār, as Nityasūris sound *tiruccinnam* for Perumāḷ.
- ***Āṇmin vānagam***: **"Rule our heaven!"** (*āḷ-min*, "you rule"). The apsarases of that world ask him to be *their* king, as the people of Ayodhyā longed to live under Rāma's rule. Why would Nammāḻvār rule? The Lord has handed him all of *nitya* and *līlā vibhūti* (see next); Brahmā's world is part of it. "We would be happy to live under your rule."
- **"I'm a servant, not a ruler"** (his imagined reply) — their answer: ***Āzhiyān tamar***: "you are the devotee of the Lord who holds the cakra, whose beauty has conquered you."
- ***Vāḷ oṇ kaṇ maḍandhaiyar***: *vāḷ* = brilliant, *oṇ* = beautiful, *maḍandhaiyar* = young women (apsarases). They **widen their eyes** (so that the light and beauty of the eyes shine out). Nampiḷḷai's image: **a mother's eyes widen when her son who went abroad returns.**
- ***Vāzhttinar magizhndē*** — they blessed (sang *pallāṇḍu*) **joyfully**, not as a duty. Nampiḷḷai's contrast: a flight attendant says "hello"/"bye" as *duty*; these did it *with joy*. And the consolation: those who mocked you on earth cannot imagine the status of those who **bless you here**.

## Pasuram 7 — *Maḍandhaiyar vāzhttalum*
**Reason given: Saulabhya (accessibility) and Saushīlya (gracious condescension) — "Kuḍandai Kōvalan".**

**Verse (from recitation):**
மடந்தையர் வாழ்த்தலும் மருதரும் வசுக்களும்
தொடர்ந்தெங்கும் தோத்திரம் சொல்லினர் தொடுகடல்
கிடந்தவெம் கேசவன் கிளரொளி மணிமுடி
குடந்தையெம் கோவலன் குடியடியார்க்கே

**Deeper explanation**
- **The two qualities.** *Saulabhya* = being easily approachable: the Lord rests nearby at **Kumbakōṇam (Tirukkuḍandai)**, where devotees can come and serve Him. *Saushīlya* = *nīrmai*, mixing with all in simplicity: as a cowherd (*kōvalan* = Gopāla) mixing with the calves. One who is both near and gentle will not sit in Vaikuṇṭha and wait; He comes and takes the soul by the hand.
- **Stage:** the soul has crossed all twelve lokas and is on the bank of the **Virajā**. The Ātivāhikas can go **only up to the Virajā** — they still hold posts and have not attained moksha. The apsarases of Satyaloka are still blessing (*maḍandhaiyar vāzhttalum*: the blessing of verse 6 has not ended).
- ***Marudar*** = the **49 Maruts**; ***Vasus*** = the **eight Vasus**. They "follow" (*todarndu*) as far as their boundary allows, **leaping from world to world** at the ālvār's own speed — "because the Lord is waiting impatiently in Vaikuṇṭha and any delay would be *apacāram*" (Nampiḷḷai). They want at least to see the ālvār's *tīrthavāri* at the Virajā. Dr. Venkatesh links it to the Vaikāsi-Viśākha *tīrthavāri* at Āḻvār Tirunagari.
- ***Toḍu-kaḍal kiḍanda em Kēśavan***: two readings. (a) *toḍu-kaḍal* = the deep sea of milk. (b) *toḍu-kaḍal* = "**dug** sea" — the oceans dug by Sagara's sons (*sāgara*). **Kēśavan** = (*ka* = Brahmā, *īśa* = Śiva): "He who gives rise to Brahmā and Śiva". He rests on the milk ocean to **protect the devas**.
- ***Kiḷar oḷi maṇi muḍi Kuḍandai em Kōvalan***: in Kumbakōṇam He is Śārṅgapāṇi / Ārāvamudu, **shining with a gem-set crown**, yet called "*Kōvalan*" (cowherd). Nampiḷḷai: one who wants to see Kṛṣṇa's cowherd simplicity **should come to Kumbakōṇam**. The *kuḍiyaḍiyār* = devotees by **generations** (*kuḍi*), conquered by that simplicity.
- **Why does the ālvār end at Kumbakōṇam?** The last place he sings before Vaikuṇṭha (his Tiruvāymozhi on Kumbakōṇam is his sixth decad there). Elders say: (1) Nammāḻvār, omniscient, knew **Nāthamunigaḷ** would hear his decad on Kumbakōṇam and revive the 4,000 Prabandhams — so he sang that Lord before leaving; (2) **Tirumaṅgai Āzhvār**, who follows his way, *also* ends at Kumbakōṇam (Tirunedunthāṇḍakam: "*Aṉṟāyar kulamagaḷukku…*").

## Pasuram 8 — *Kuḍiyaḍiyār ivar Gōvindan*
**Reason given: "Gōvindan" — the Lord who shepherds the cows; we are His cattle who cannot find the pasture, and He leads us to the best pasture, Vaikuṇṭha.**

**Verse (from recitation):**
குடியடியார் இவர் கோவிந்தன் தனக்கென்று
முடியுடை வானவர் முறைமுறை எதிர்கொள்ள
கொடியணி நெடுமதிள் கோபுரம் குறுகினர்
வடிவுடை மாதவன் வைகுந்தம் புகவே

**Deeper explanation**
- **Transition:** verses 1–7 were honours from beyond Vaikuṇṭha. From here (8), the honours are **from within Vaikuṇṭha**. The soul has bathed in the Virajā and stands before the gopura. The Nityasūris and Muktas come out to the gate.
- **Gōvindan** (*go* = cows): cows do not find good pasture themselves; the cowherd leads them. We are like cows, lacking knowledge. The Lord takes us to the choicest pasture, Vaikuṇṭha, and gives His experience as grazing.
- ***Kuḍiyaḍiyār***: *kuḍi* = lineage. They say: "Here comes one whose **whole line** has signed away to Govinda." (Periyāzhvār: *ennāḷ emperumān unnadiyēn enru ezhuttuppaṭṭa annāḷē*, he and his lineage written down as servants.)
- **A nuance.** Some in Vaikuṇṭha enjoy the Lord's *vibhava* (incarnations), some His *arcā* (temple forms), besides Vaikuṇṭhanātha. So in verse 7 the Ātivāhikas praised Nammāḻvār's love for the *arcā* at Kumbakōṇam, while the Nityasūris here praise his love for the *vibhava* — **Kṛṣṇa**, to whom the name Gōvinda belongs specially.
- ***Muḍiyuḍai vānavar***: "the heavenly ones with crowns". These are **Nityasūris and Muktas**, not devas. Why crowns, when the Lord alone rules? He wears a crown for the **kingdom of protection and qualities**; they wear crowns for the **kingdom of service** (*kaiṅkarya sāmrājya*). Dr. Venkatesh's image: a *ṭavāli* (the collector's attendant) with a cap — it is a mark of the *service*, not of rulership.
- ***Muṟai muṟai*** = in proper protocol: Viṣvaksena, Ādiśeṣa and Garuḍa first, then the Muktas.
- ***Koḍi aṇi neḍu madil gōpuram kuṟuginar***: the tall wall, golden and *aprākṛta* (not of this world's matter), **decked with flags** (*koḍi aṇi*) — decorated **only now** for this arrival. *Kuṟuginar* = "went near/approached".
- ***Vaḍivuḍai Mādhavan Vaikuntham pugavē***: *vaḍivu* = beauty/youthfulness. At the *mere news* that the ālvār is coming, Perumāḷ and Thāyār **become more beautiful and young**. Illustrated by Daśaratha: after 60,000 years, seeing Rāma he became sixteen again (a Vālmīki śloka he cites); Vasudeva seeing Kṛṣṇa, "*yuveva Vasudevo'bhūt*" (Viṣṇu Purāṇa, Parāśara). They had not even seen him yet.

## Pasuram 9 — *Vaikuntham pugudalum*
**Reason given: "Vaikuṇṭha" itself — *vi-kuṇṭha* = without obstruction (kuṇṭha). He gives the experience without a single break, so He starts giving it as soon as the soul leaves the body.**

**Verse (from recitation; one reading flagged below):**
வைகுந்தம் புகுதலும் வாசலில் வானவர்
வைகுந்தன் தமரெமர் எமதிடம் புகுதென்று
வைகுந்தத்து அமரரும் முனிவரும் வியந்தனர்
வைகுந்தம் புகுவது மண்ணவர் விதியே
*(Flag: the caption reads "maṉṉavar" in Dr. Venkatesh's reading; the last lines of the caption show "maṇṇavar". "Maṇṇavar" (those of the earth) is the sense he explains — please confirm in print.)*

**Deeper explanation**
- **Why "Vaikuṇṭha"?** *Kuṇṭha* = obstacle; *vi-kuṇṭha* = place of no obstacle. The Lord wants no break in the experience. He gives the *arcā* experience in this life, the *glimpse* on the path, and unending *kaiṅkarya* at Vaikuṇṭha. So He walks with us from the moment we leave the body.
- ***Vāsalil vānavar***: the **dvārapālakas** (gatekeepers) — Jaya and Vijaya at one gate, Kumuda and Kumudākṣa, Nanda and Sunanda, and a fourth pair (the caption reads *Dhātā, Vidhātā*); four gates. They are also Nityasūris.
- ***Vaikuntan tamar emar*** = "a devotee of the Lord of Vaikuṇṭha is **our master**" (*emar*). Compare Nammāḻvār's own *payilum tiruvuṭaiyār evarēlum avar kaṇḍīr… emmai āḷum paramarē* and Tirumaṅgai Āzhvār's *Tirucchērai … tāḷ toḻuvār kāṇmin en talaimēlārē*.
- ***Emadidam pugudu***: *idam* = position/post. They **hand him their staff (*pirambu*)**: "take our place". (A note on Jaya-Vijaya: they stopped the Sanatkumāras and were cursed. Dr. Venkatesh explains that was **Kārya Vaikuṇṭha** — a replica (like a branch office of TTD in T. Nagar) — not the real Nitya Vaikuṇṭha; the gatekeepers' *posts* are held by many, and the Nitya Vaikuṇṭha ones are not at fault.)
- ***Amarar* and *munivar***: two groups in Vaikuṇṭha who live in harmony: **amarar** are the *kaiṅkarya-niṣṭhas* (devoted to service), **munivar** the *guṇānubhava-niṣṭhas* (absorbed in contemplating the Lord's qualities; *manana-śīlaḥ munih*). (He cites the śloka *Vaikuṇṭhe tu pare loke Śriyā sārdhaṁ jagat-patiḥ… bhaktaiḥ bhāgavataiḥ saha*.)
- ***Vaikuntham puguvadu maṇṇavar vidhiyē***: the **most misread line**. Popular meaning: "everyone born on earth must go to Vaikuṇṭha one day — it is *fate* (*vidhi*)." **Dr. Venkatesh insists this is wrong**: (1) not everyone goes; only the *prapanna* (or the *bhakti-yogi*); (2) the *ācāryas* did not interpret it so; (3) *vidhi* here means ***bhāgya* (good fortune)**, and it is **whose fortune?** The sentence continues from "the amarar and munivar *marvelled*": it is the **fortune of the Vaikuṇṭha-dwellers** that a soul *from earth* comes to them.
- **The rasokti** (underlined as "for the sake of a taste", not doctrine): **earth-dwellers enjoy the Lord in the arcā temples in ways Vaikuṇṭha cannot offer** — you can bind Kṛṣṇa to a mortar and scold Him; you cannot do that to Vaikuṇṭhanātha. So when someone who has relished all that comes to Vaikuṇṭha, they say: "what fortune for us" — as we say when "a devotee from Śrīraṅgam joins our group".

## Pasuram 10 — *Vidhi vagai pugundanar*
**Reason given: Perumāḷ has *appointed 500 attendants* for each incoming soul — the proof of His affection (*abhimāna*).**

**Verse (from recitation):**
விதிவகை புகுந்தனர் என்று நல்வேதியர்
பதியினிற் பாங்கினிற் பாதங்கள் கழுவினர்
நிதியும் நற்சுண்ணமும் நிறைகுட விளக்கமும்
மதிமுக மடந்தையர் ஏந்தினர் வந்தே

**Deeper explanation**
- **500 apsarases** (*madhi-muga maḍandhaiyar*, **of Vaikuṇṭha, not of the devas**) are appointed *for each jīva*. "Do not read this worldly-royal." They are attendants of Lakṣmī, and they themselves bow to the Mukta as they lead him in.
- ***Vidhi vagai pugundanar***: *vidhi* = *bhāgya* again. "By **our** collective good fortune he has entered." The speakers are ***nal vēdiyar***. Nampiḷḷai: not "those who study Veda well" but **"those *praised by* the Veda"**. The Veda names the Nityasūris: *te ha nākam mahimānaḥ sacante yatra pūrve sādhyāḥ santi devāḥ*; *sadā paśyanti sūrayaḥ tad viṣṇoḥ paramaṁ padam*; *ṛṣayaḥ prathamajāḥ ye purāṇāḥ*. They have **no scent of karma or rebirth**, and they say: "the one who sang the Tamil Veda (Nammāḻvār) came to us by our good fortune."
- ***Padiyiniṟ pāṅginiṟ pādangaḷ kazhuvinar***: *padi* = residence/palace. Each Nityasūri — **Ādiśeṣa, Garuḍa, Viṣvaksena** — has his own *tirumāḷigai* in Vaikuṇṭha, given by the Lord's grace. Each invites him: "Come to my palace." *Pāṅgu* = due honour. They seat him on a great seat, wash his feet, perform *tirumañjanam*, and **sprinkle that Śrīpāda-tīrtham on their heads** ("what fortune that we get the ālvār's tīrtham").
- **The four offerings** of the apsarases:
  1. **Nidhi** = wealth = **the Lord's *pādukā*** (*tiruvaḍi-nilai*). Before one meets a great person you honour his sandals first. *Question*: Nammāḻvār is himself Perumāḷ's pādukā (Śaṭhāri) — how can he be honoured with a pādukā? Dr. Venkatesh: Perumāḷ has *many* pādukās; or the Nityasūris take many forms. "If we go into research, we lose the *anubhava*; keep the faith that the Lord has a way, and 99% of doubts go."
  2. **Nal-chuṇṇam** = *Śrī Cūrṇam*, the powder worn on the forehead.
  3. **Nirai-kuḍam** = *pūrṇa-kumbham*. Earlier the devas used clouds; here it is the real one, pure-*sattvam*, *aprākṛta*.
  4. **Viḷakkam** = *maṅgaḷa dīpam*.
- ***Madhi-mukha***: faces **like the (full) moon**, because they are in Lakṣmī's place, as a mother's face shines like the full moon when her child returns from abroad. They say "our Mahālakṣmī's *child* has come".
- **Cliffhanger** in the discourse: someone special will meet him at the Tirumāmaṇi Maṇḍapam — the next verse.

## Pasuram 11 — *Vandu avar edhirkoḷḷa* (the phalashruti)
**Reason given: Perumāḷ Himself comes with Thāyār to receive him.**

**Verse (from recitation, with Dr. Venkatesh's splitting):**
வந்தவர் எதிர்கொள்ள மாமணி மண்டபத்து
அந்தமில் பேரின்பத்து அடியரோடு இருந்தமை
கொந்தலர் பொழில் குருகூர்ச் சடகோபன்
சொல் சந்தங்கள் ஆயிரத்து இவை வல்லார் முனிவரே
*(He reads "vandu avar edhirkoḷḷa" — see below.)*

**Deeper explanation**
- **The split reading.** *"Vandavar edhirkoḷḷa"* could mean "those who had already come received him". Dr. Venkatesh splits it: ***"vandu avar edhirkoḷḷa"*** — **"He (*avar*) Himself came and received"**. *Avar* is the Lord with Piraṭṭi. Sending the Nityasūris was *not enough* for Him. Perumāḷ (seated on Ādiśeṣa with Thāyār) **rose, came to the gate of the Tirumāmaṇi Maṇḍapam**, and said: "Kurukūr Śaṭhakōpa! We waited only for your arrival; we rejoice at your coming."
- **Who led him on the path?** Dr. Venkatesh: **Kāḷamēgha Perumāḷ of Tirumōhūr** (caption: "Tirumūkūr") was the one who accompanied the ālvār the whole way (to be understood later in Tiruvāymozhi). I note this is *his* aside, not elaborated.
- **The reversed search.** The ālvār wept for the Lord throughout Tiruvāymozhi (*kūvikoḷḷum kālam innum kuṟugādō*, *iruḷ tarum mā ñālattu ini piṟavi yān vēṇḍēn*). Now the Lord who said "*vāsudevaḥ sarvam iti sa mahātmā sudurlabhaḥ*" (Bhagavad Gītā: that great soul who sees Vāsudeva as everything is very rare) finds, in the ālvār (*uṇṇum sōṟu parugu nīr tinnum veṟṟilaiyum kaṇṇan*), the very devotee He had sought. **"Piriṇdavar kūḍinār pēsal vēṇḍumō"** — when those parted meet, what need to speak?
- **Tirumāmaṇi Maṇḍapam**: a thousand-pillared hall (like the one at Śrīraṅgam, with a few differences Dr. Venkatesh says he will mention later).
- **He takes his hand and places His two feet on his head** — which also fulfils pasuram 3's "foot placed on every head" at last.
- ***Andamil pērinbam***: *andam* = end; *pērinbam* = great bliss. Worldly pleasures are either mixed with sorrow, are small (*ciṟṟinbam*), or have an end. This one has **no end and no mixture of sorrow**; it lasts as long as the jīva (*yāvad ātma-bhāvitvāt*, per the śāstra he quotes).
- ***Adiyarōḍu irundamai***: the ālvār's greatest wish was *not only* to be with the Lord but **with His devotees** (*Nadumāṟkaḍimai*, and *kazhippum kavar maṟṟu… oḷi koṇḍa sōdiyumāy uḍan kūḍuvadu enṟukolō… aḻikkinṟa māyappirān aḍiyār kuzhāṅgaḷaiyē*). He sits among the Nityasūris and Muktas, serving them.
- ***Kondalar pozhil Kurugūr Śaṭhagōpan***: *kondu* = bunch, *alar* = blossoming; *pozhil* = grove. As a whole bouquet blooms at once, in Vaikuṇṭha **the jñāna of every liberated soul blooms 100%** (*"jñāna vikāsa"*). Nampiḷḷai adds: the Lord says Vaikuṇṭha should have groves like Āḻvār Tirunagari's. (Dr. Venkatesh asks us to face south, toward Āḻvār Tirunagari, whenever the name *Kurugūr Śaṭhakōpan* appears.)
- ***Sol sandangaḷ āyirattu***: *sandam* also means **the Veda** (*chandas*), so these thousand verses are **Veda in Tamil form**. The Archirādhi path is described in the Chāndogya, Bṛhadāraṇyaka and Kauṣītakī Upaniṣads — it cannot be "sung" by anyone, only by one with the Lord's grace. "Don't take it as mere literature."
- ***Ivai vallār munivarē*** (the phala-śruti): those who can recite these ten become ***munivar*** — *not* hermits, but like the *munis* of pasuram 9: those absorbed in the Lord's *guṇa-anubhava* (beauty, qualities, ornaments, weapons, līlās). By hearing these verses, the desire to go the Archirādhi path arises, then reflection on the Lord who leads, and gradually constant contemplation. They will taste **in this life** what the Nityasūris taste. *Moksha follows as a matter of course* ("it goes without saying").
- **Epilogue** (Dr. Venkatesh): when Nammāḻvār opens his eyes he is still at Tirunagari under the tamarind tree. He cries "Haven't I reached Vaikuṇṭha?" and begins the next decad (*Muniyē Nānmuganē*), "what was shown to the mind's eye, show to the outer eye". The Lord comes with even greater longing.
