JH-5.07 · Chapter 5: The Bestower of Grace

Name 43 of 54

धाता தாதா Dhātā

The One who sows and creates, planting the seed of all creation.

Acharya's Teaching

Dhātā means the One who sows. Dr. Venkatesh teaches that just as Rāma sowed Mārīca so the epic could flourish, before creation the Lord wished the world to spread into many branches, and sowed Brahmā as a seed in the primal matter (mūla prakṛti), so that all creation could grow through him.

The Story

Dr. Venkatesh tells of Viśvāmitra's sacrifice, which Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa guarded, as Kamban sings, the way eyelids guard the eye. He notes the elders' reading: Rāma was the upper lid, taller and moving about, while Lakṣmaṇa was the lower lid, steady in one place. The lid closes before anything touches the eye, just as they met every danger before it arrived.

For six days all was quiet. On the seventh, Mārīca and Subāhu rained flesh and blood on the sacrificial ground. Rāma killed Subāhu but only hurled Mārīca into the sea. Why spare him? Dr. Venkatesh cites Villur Swamy's verse in his Mañju Rāmāyaṇa: the story of Rāma is a wish-fulfilling tree, and Rāma wanted that tree to grow. Had Mārīca died then, there would be no golden deer, no abduction of Sītā, no great epic. So Rāma planted Mārīca like a seed in the sea, and from it the Rāmāyaṇa grew.

Living This Divine Name

Some things that seem to be left unfinished are in fact seeds being planted for a larger growth later.

Today's Reflection & Practice

Name one unfinished thing in your life and ask what it might be growing toward.

Chant

Om Dhātrē Namaḥa

Connection to the Journey Home

Even what looked like a pause in the story was a seed. On the journey home, no delay is wasted in His hands.
