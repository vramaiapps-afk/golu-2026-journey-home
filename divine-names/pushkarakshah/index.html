JH-5.04 · Chapter 5: The Bestower of Grace

Name 40 of 54

पुष्कराक्षः புஷ்கராக்ஷஃ Puṣkarākṣaḥ

The One whose lotus eyes captivate every heart.

Acharya's Teaching

Puṣkara means lotus and akṣa means eye. Dr. Venkatesh teaches that if those eyes could hold even a rākṣasī so completely, no devotee can fail to be drawn by them. Through His glance (katākṣa) He removes the devotee's every shortcoming and draws them to Himself.

The Story

Dr. Venkatesh picks up the question left at the end of the last episode. In Pañcavaṭi, Śurpaṇakhā, struck by Rāma's beauty, asked him to marry her. Rāma sent her to Lakṣmaṇa; Lakṣmaṇa, calling himself Rāma's servant, sent her back. Believing Sītā was the obstacle, she rushed to devour her, and at Rāma's word Lakṣmaṇa drew his sword and cut off her nose and ears.

The puzzle: Śurpaṇakhā was a powerful, shape-changing rākṣasī. Why did she just stand there? Dr. Venkatesh says Vālmīki answers it: she never saw Lakṣmaṇa or the blade. Her eyes were fixed on Rāma's lotus eyes, and she stood spellbound. Even afterward, weeping in front of Rāvaṇa, she described the brothers as puṇḍarīka viśālākṣau, with wide lotus eyes. Even mutilated, it was their eyes she remembered.

Living This Divine Name

What we look at shapes us. A single glance at what is truly beautiful can hold the heart more firmly than anything else.

Today's Reflection & Practice

Rest your eyes today for one minute on something beautiful and offer that looking to Him.

Chant

Om Puṣkarākṣāya Namaḥa

Connection to the Journey Home

The first sight of His eyes is what turns the soul from all else; on the journey home, His glance is the very thing that draws it forward.
