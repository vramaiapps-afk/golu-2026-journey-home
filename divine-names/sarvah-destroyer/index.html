JH-3.08 · Chapter 3: The Protector

Name 26 of 54

शर्वः ஶர்வஃ Śarvaḥ

The One who destroys sin and sorrow at their root.

Acharya's Teaching

From the root śru — to destroy — comes śarvan, the destroyer of sins and sorrows. Dr. Venkatesh distinguishes this from the previous name, Sarvaḥ (having everything as His body): this name, śarvaḥ, is specifically about destruction of affliction at its root. Though Śiva himself carries the name Śarvan too, it is Narayana — who removed even Śiva's own sorrow — who is praised as śarvaḥ in the Sahasranāma.

The Story

Dr. Venkatesh tells of a time when both Brahmā and Śiva had five heads each. When Brahmā's fifth head spoke arrogantly, Śiva angrily plucked it off — but the skull stuck permanently to Śiva's own hand, along with the sin of Brahmahatti (harming a Brahmin figure). Unable to free himself despite visiting countless holy waters and worlds, Śiva eventually wanders, as a mendicant, to Thirukkandiyur, where Perumāḷ resides as "Hara Śapa Vimochana Perumāḷ."

Bathing in the temple's Kamala Puṣkariṇi tank and praying, Śiva receives Perumāḷ's grace: with Mother Kamalāmbikā's intercession, Perumāḷ Kamalānāthan casts His glance at the skull, instantly destroying the sin and freeing it from Śiva's hand — complete śāpa vimocanam. Āḷvār praises this event, calling the Lord of Kandiyūr the medicine (marunthu) that removes even the gravest sin.

Living This Divine Name

No burden is too old or too deeply stuck to be released — even the mightiest among us sometimes need this same grace.

Today's Reflection & Practice

Name one thing you're still carrying, and consciously set it down today.

Chant

Om Śarvāya Namaḥa

Connection to the Journey Home

If even Śiva's ancient affliction could be destroyed at Thirukkandiyūr, no burden the soul carries on its journey home is too heavy to be released before reaching Him.
