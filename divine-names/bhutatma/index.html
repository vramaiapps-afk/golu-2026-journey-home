JH-1.08 · Chapter 1: The Supreme Lord

Name 8 of 54

भूतात्मा பூதாத்மா Bhūtātmā

The Self who dwells within every created being and thing.

Acharya's Teaching

Bhūta here means all created things; ātmā means the indwelling soul. Because every element — earth, water, fire, air, sky — and every jivātma is His body, Dr. Venkatesh explains a striking consequence: harm done to one part of that body is felt in another. Cut down the earth's trees, and the sky withholds its rain in anger; overbuild the land, and the sea rises in anger and sends a tsunami — because both land and sea are the same body, His body, and a strike on one part is felt in the other.

The Story

Dr. Venkatesh tells of King Janaka's great assembly, where the sage Yājñavalkya answers every question put to him by rival sages, winning the prize of 500 cows. Finally, the sage Uddālaka asks the decisive question: "Who dwells within and supports all beings and all things?"

Yājñavalkya answers: Nārāyaṇa — dwelling within the earth, the sky, and every element as their inner support, unknown to them, yet essential to their existence. Because all creation is His body, he explains, harm in one place is felt elsewhere: cut down the earth's trees, and the sky withholds rain; overbuild the land, and the sea rises in anger. Every individual soul, too, is His body, with Him dwelling as its innermost Self.

Living This Divine Name

Since all creation is His body, harm done in one place is felt elsewhere — a reminder that nothing we do is ever truly isolated.

Today's Reflection & Practice

Today, treat one part of the natural world around you as part of His body.

Chant

Om Bhūtātmane Namaḥa

Connection to the Journey Home

The jivātma making the journey home is itself a body to Him — the same closeness Yājñavalkya described is what welcomes the soul at every stage.
