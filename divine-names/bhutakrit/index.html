JH-1.05 · Chapter 1: The Supreme Lord

Name 5 of 54

भूतकृत् பூதக்ருத் Bhūtakṛt

The One who is, by Himself, all three causes behind creation.

Acharya's Teaching

Every ordinary object needs three distinct causes: an upādāna kāraṇa (material cause — the dosa batter), a nimitta kāraṇa (efficient cause, the maker — the cook), and a sahakāri kāraṇa (instrumental cause — the griddle and tools). Dr. Venkatesh's answer to why the universe needs only one cause: like a spider that draws its web's material from its own body, is the web's maker, and uses its own body as the instrument, Perumāḷ alone is simultaneously the material, the maker, and the means of all creation. Kṛt means the one who causes or creates.

The Story

Dr. Venkatesh tells of a conference on Kailāśa where sages ask Śiva how many causes the universe has. Every ordinary object needs three: a material cause, a maker, and tools — a dosa needs batter, a cook, and a griddle.

Śiva answers: for the universe as a whole, there is only one cause — Nārāyaṇa alone. Like a spider spinning its web from its own body, Perumāḷ is simultaneously the material, the maker, and the means of the entire creation.

Living This Divine Name

Nothing we create is ever truly separate from us — in the same way, all creation remains inseparable from Him.

Today's Reflection & Practice

Notice one thing you made or grew today, and see your own hand in it as He sees His.

Chant

Om Bhūtakṛtāya Namaḥa

Connection to the Journey Home

Since He is the source of all creation, the soul returning to Him is simply returning to its own origin.
