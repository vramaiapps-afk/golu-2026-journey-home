JH-3.05 · Chapter 3: The Protector

Name 23 of 54

केशवः கேஶவஃ Keśavaḥ

The One whose beauty served and protected His devotee in a moment of need.

Acharya's Teaching

Keśam means hair. Dr. Venkatesh notes that this name doesn't celebrate physical beauty for its own sake — it commemorates a specific act of protection, where Perumāḷ personally intervened, taking on an unusual and specific form (long hair, matching the priest's claim exactly) purely to save a devotee from unjust punishment. The deity at Thirukkaṇṇapuram is still called “Saurirāja Perumāḷ” in honor of this event.

The Story

Dr. Venkatesh tells of a temple priest at Thirukkaṇṇapuram who secretly diverted some of King Saraboji's garland offerings to his lover instead of the deity. The king, hearing rumors, visits in disguise to investigate. Unaware, the priest retrieves the very garlands his lover had already worn and re-offers them to the deity — and when served as prasādam, the disguised king finds a long strand of a woman's hair among the flowers, confirming his suspicion and revealing himself.

The priest insists the hair is Perumāḷ's own — the king demands proof, permitting him to view the deity from behind during the next day's procession, threatening punishment if unproven. The priest prays desperately to Perumāḷ. The next day, during the Amāvāsyā procession, Perumāḷ Himself appears with long, flowing hair — vindicating the priest's claim and sparing him the king's punishment.

Living This Divine Name

He steps in personally to protect those who serve Him, even in the smallest, most unexpected ways — down to a detail as specific as a strand of hair.

Today's Reflection & Practice

Defend someone today who can't defend themselves.

Chant

Om Keśavāya Namaḥa

Connection to the Journey Home

This name shows a Lord who intervenes in exact, specific detail for a devotee in trouble — the same attentiveness that accompanies the soul through each precise stage of its journey home.
