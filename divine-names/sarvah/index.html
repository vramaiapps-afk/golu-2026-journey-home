JH-3.07 · Chapter 3: The Protector

Name 25 of 54

सर्वः ஸர்வஃ Sarvaḥ

The One who has the entire universe as His own body.

Acharya's Teaching

Sarvam means everything. Dr. Venkatesh draws the analogy directly: a single mouth eating nourishes an entire body without every organ needing to eat separately, because the body is one interconnected whole. In the same way, because the entire universe and every being in it is Perumāḷ's own body, His single act reaches and satisfies all of it at once — hence Sarvaḥ.

The Story

Dr. Venkatesh tells of Śrīnivāsa Kalyāṇam at Tirumala — unusually named after the groom rather than the bride, because Śrīnivāsa personally sponsored the entire wedding, borrowing from Kubera to feed 33 crore devas, 66 crore asuras, and countless sages. He appoints Agni as cook and uses the temple's sacred tanks as cooking vessels.

Worried about the delay of feeding such an enormous crowd before the procession can depart, Perumāḷ Himself sits in the front row and eats everything meant for everyone. The assembled sages, initially offended that the groom ate before them, soon find themselves completely satisfied without having eaten at all. When Brahmā asks how this is possible, Perumāḷ explains: just as only the mouth eats, yet nourishment reaches every part of the body, the entire universe is His body — so His eating alone satisfies every being within it.

Living This Divine Name

What nourishes the whole eventually reaches every part — care given anywhere ripples everywhere, even where we can't see it land.

Today's Reflection & Practice

Do one act of care today, trusting it reaches further than you can see.

Chant

Om Sarvāya Namaḥa

Connection to the Journey Home

Just as one act at Śrīnivāsa's wedding nourished every guest across every world, the Lord's grace on the soul's journey home reaches every stage at once, not one at a time.
