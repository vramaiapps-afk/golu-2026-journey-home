JH-2.04 · Chapter 2: The Compassionate Lord

Name 13 of 54

अव्ययः அவ்யயஃ Avyayaḥ

The One who holds His devotees close and never lets them slip away.

Acharya's Teaching

Vyaya means to slip away or dissipate; Avyayaḥ means the One who does not let His devotees slip away — who holds them firmly within Himself. Unlike Indra, whose jealousy of an equal caused him to cast Yayāti down, Perumāḷ is delighted, not threatened, when His devotees rise toward becoming like Him, and He never lets go of anyone who reaches Him.

The Story

Dr. Venkatesh tells of King Yayāti, whose many acts of charity earned him a seat in heaven equal to Indra's own. Jealous, Indra schemes to bring him down: while celestial dancers perform, he goads Yayāti into boasting about his own generosity. The moment Yayāti's pride peaks — claiming no one in the world has given more than him — he begins falling from his seat. Bṛhaspati explains: the fall began the instant the pride did.

Yayāti lands in the yajña-hall of his own grandson, Pratardana, who explains that Indra acts this way only out of jealousy toward anyone who becomes his equal — and that Yayāti should have sought Nārāyaṇa's feet instead. When Yayāti asks whether Nārāyaṇa would cast him down too, Pratardana answers: Perumāḷ has no such jealousy — He delights when His devotees become His equals in eight divine qualities, and never pushes anyone away. Yayāti meditates on Perumāḷ, practices bhakti yoga, and attains mokṣa — from where, as the Upaniṣad says, none ever return.

Living This Divine Name

Growth and closeness to Him are never threats to Him — only causes for His joy.

Today's Reflection & Practice

Celebrate someone else's progress today without any hint of comparison.

Chant

Om Avyayāya Namaḥa

Connection to the Journey Home

The soul making its way home can trust that once it reaches Him, unlike Yayāti in Indra's court, it will never be cast down or let slip away again.
