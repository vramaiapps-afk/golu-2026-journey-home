JH-5.01 · Chapter 5: The Bestower of Grace

Name 37 of 54

स्वयम्भूः ஸ்வயம்பூஃ Svayambhūḥ

The One who appears in the divine form of His own choosing.

Acharya's Teaching

Svayambhūḥ means the One who appears as He wishes. Dr. Venkatesh explains the Mothers' point: we are born in bodies of the five elements, shaped by our karma, but when Perumāḷ descends He takes a divine form made of pure sattva (pañca-upaniṣad-maya), entirely by His own will.

The Story

Dr. Venkatesh tells of a brāhmaṇa who came to Kṛṣṇa's court in Dvārakā, protesting that the kingdom was ill-governed: five of his children had vanished into the sky the moment they were born, and his wife was expecting a sixth. Arjuna, stung by the charge, vowed to protect the child and built a cage of arrows from Gāṇḍīva around the mother. The child was born — and vanished all the same. Bound by his vow, Arjuna prepared to enter the fire.

Kṛṣṇa stopped him, took Arjuna and the brāhmaṇa into His chariot, and had it driven straight toward Vaikuṇṭha. There, on the laps of Śrīdevī, Bhūdevī and Nīḷādevī, sat all six children. The Mothers explained: on earth He had taken a body unlike ours, and they had longed to see it, so they had staged this līlā to bring Him. Kṛṣṇa gently asked them to stop, returned the children to the brāhmaṇa, and brought him home to Dvārakā. Dr. Venkatesh cites Āḷvār's verse in the Tiruvāymoṛi on how He restored the brāhmaṇa's sons.

Living This Divine Name

Our bodies come to us shaped by our past; His form is chosen out of love alone. That difference is the whole difference between bondage and grace.

Today's Reflection & Practice

Pause once today and give thanks for your body, then remember it is only a loan for the journey.

Chant

Om Svayambhavē Namaḥa

Connection to the Journey Home

As we begin Chapter 5, the Lord who needs no body of karma comes among us in a form of His own choosing, and that same freedom is what He holds out to the soul at the end of its journey home.
