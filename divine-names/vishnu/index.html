JH-1.02 · Chapter 1: The Supreme Lord

Name 2 of 54

विष्णुः விஷ்ணு: Viṣṇuḥ

The One who enters and fills everything — the universe is His body, and He dwells within it as the Self.

Acharya's Teaching

The word Viṣṇu comes from the root viṣ, to enter and pervade completely. Because the entire universe and every being within it is His body, and He dwells inside as the indwelling Self (antarātmā), nothing and no one stands truly outside Him. Dr. Venkatesh draws out the implication directly: separation from another being is, at the deepest level, an illusion — even someone we consider an enemy shares the same indwelling Lord we do.

The Story

Dr. Venkatesh tells of the cardamom milk (ēlakkāi pāl) — a delicacy Kṛṣṇa had never stolen before, so curiosity got the better of Him. He sneaks into the gopi Puṣṭimati's house to taste it, is caught, and struck on the back with a cane.

But it is not Kṛṣṇa who cries out in pain — it is the very woman who struck Him, along with every being in creation, from an ant to the celestials to Brahmā himself in Satyaloka. As Piḷḷai Perumāḷ Aiyaṅgār's verse puts it, when she struck Him, all fourteen worlds shook with the blow. The reason: the entire universe and every being in it is His body, and He is the soul residing within. The name Viṣṇu comes from the root viṣ — to enter — the One who enters and completely fills every single thing, everywhere.

Living This Divine Name

Remembering this dissolves hatred and conflict — we're never truly separate from the one we're upset with.

Today's Reflection & Practice

Notice one moment of irritation today, and remember He dwells in them too.

Chant

Om Viṣṇave Namaḥa
