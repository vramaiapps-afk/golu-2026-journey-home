JH-2.01 · Chapter 2: The Compassionate Lord

Name 10 of 54

पूतात्मा பூதாத்மா Pūtātmā

The Pure Self who purifies whatever is offered to Him.

Acharya's Teaching

Pūta means pure; ātmā means self. Because Perumāḷ is Himself pure by nature, He purifies and elevates whatever touches Him or is offered to Him — even something as plain and “incomplete” as unsalted food becomes, in His hands, more delicious than amuta (nectar). The name teaches that it is His purity acting on the offering, not the offering's own richness, that makes it sacred.

The Story

Dr. Venkatesh tells the story behind Uppiliappan Kovil, near Kumbakonam. Perumāḷ once promised to accept only unsalted offerings there, and kept that word so seriously that even today, every prasādam offered to Uppiliappan is prepared entirely without salt.

By Tamil proverb, food without salt belongs in the rubbish — tasteless, incomplete. Yet Dr. Venkatesh explains that because Uppiliappan is Himself Pūtātmā, the Pure Self, He makes even this saltless offering into something sweeter than nectar for those who partake of it. Purity, not richness, is what transforms the offering.

Living This Divine Name

What matters isn't the richness of what we offer, but the purity of heart behind it.

Today's Reflection & Practice

Offer one small, humble thing today, without worrying whether it's “enough.”

Chant

Om Pūtātmane Namaḥa

Connection to the Journey Home

Just as Uppiliappan purifies even a saltless offering, the soul's own imperfect offerings are made pure and worthy on the journey home.
