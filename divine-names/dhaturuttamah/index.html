JH-5.09 · Chapter 5: The Bestower of Grace

Name 45 of 54

धातुरुत्तमः தாதுருத்தமஃ Dhāturuttamaḥ

The foremost Creator, greater than Brahmā himself.

Acharya's Teaching

Dhātu is the creator, here Brahmā, and uttamaḥ means the highest. Dr. Venkatesh points out the secret: Brahmā made Ahalyā out of the elements, bone, flesh and skin. But Rāma gave life afresh to a lifeless rock with the dust of His feet and renewed her, lifting a curse that even Brahmā could not remove.

The Story

Dr. Venkatesh tells that when Brahmā once set out to create the most beautiful woman in the world, the result was Ahalyā. The devas, Indra among them, all asked for her hand. Brahmā set a contest: whoever first circled the world would win her. Indra set out on his elephant Airāvata. But the sage Gautama, as a cow in his hermitage was calving, simply circled the cow, which by the śāstras equals circling the whole earth. Brahmā, impressed, gave Ahalyā to Gautama.

Indra, cheated, took the form of a cock and crowed before dawn to send Gautama off to the river, then came in Gautama's own form to Ahalyā. Gautama returned, learned the truth, and cursed them both. Ahalyā was to lie as a stone for thousands of years, until the son of Daśaratha, Rāma, came there, and the dust of His feet touched her. When Rāma passed that way with Viśvāmitra, the dust of His feet touched the stone, and Ahalyā rose again in her beauty.

Living This Divine Name

Nothing is so hardened that it cannot be softened by grace. What feels like stone in us can be brought to life.

Today's Reflection & Practice

Name one thing in you that feels stuck like stone, and offer it to Him.

Chant

Om Dhāturuttamāya Namaḥa

Connection to the Journey Home

With Dhāturuttamaḥ, Chapter 5 closes on a stone brought to life by His feet, as the soul on its journey home is raised and made new at the end.
