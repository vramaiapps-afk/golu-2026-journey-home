JH-5.03 · Chapter 5: The Bestower of Grace

Name 39 of 54

आदित्यः ஆதித்யஃ Ādityaḥ

The One who dwells within the sun as its inner Self, watching over His devotees.

Acharya's Teaching

Ādityah is the One who stands at the centre of the sun as its antaryāmi. Dr. Venkatesh explains that from there He watches over every devotee, so that no distance, a thousand miles included, stands between a cry for help and His answer. He also notes that He holds the conch and discus in hand so no time is lost when someone calls.

The Story

Dr. Venkatesh retells the well-known scene of Draupadī dragged into the Kaurava court after Dharmaraja lost her at dice. She argued that the game was invalid, since Yudhiṣṭhira had already lost himself and could not stake her. Duryodhana brushed this aside and ordered Duḥśāsana to strip her. Seeing that her husbands and the elders, Bhīṣma and Droṇa, were silent, she turned to the One refuge of the helpless and cried out: Śaṅkha cakra gadāpāṇē, Dvārakā nilaya, Acyuta, Govinda, Puṇḍarīkākṣa, rakṣa māṃ śaraṇāgatām.

At once Kṛṣṇa caused the sari to flow without end, until Duḥśāsana collapsed in exhaustion. Dr. Venkatesh says it flowed from the sun's centre, where Nārāyaṇa abides, as in dhyēyassadā savitṛmaṇḍala madhyavartī Nārāyaṇaḥ, looking down on His devotees from there. He closes with the old Tamil saying that between the 'hole-handed' (Dvārakā) and the elephant (Hastināpura) lie a thousand miles, yet a sari trade went on between them, like online shopping.

Living This Divine Name

Help is never far when it is truly called for. The distance we imagine between us and the Lord is only ours.

Today's Reflection & Practice

Next time you feel alone in a difficulty today, say His name once and trust it has been heard.

Chant

Om Ādityāya Namaḥa

Connection to the Journey Home

Draupadī was rescued across a thousand miles; the soul on its journey home is likewise watched from the sun's centre, and none of its calls go unanswered.
